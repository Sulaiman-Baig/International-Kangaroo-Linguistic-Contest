Start here: how the contest works

Built from the official IKLC course outline for Ecolier level and the 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025 papers, all 96 questions read line by line.

Contest facts

FactDetail
Questions24 multiple choice, options A to E (five choices, not four)
StoriesThree reading passages, 8 questions each
PointsQuestions 1 to 8: 3 points · 9 to 16: 4 points · 17 to 24: 5 points, plus a starting credit of 24. Maximum 120
Time120 minutes (official outline; the 2022 and 2023 booklets said 60). Plenty of time: read slowly

Study plan

1One skill a day for about two weeks. Read the skill together, then do its mini quiz.
2Then one practice paper every one or two days. Read every explanation, even for right answers.
3Last few days: two papers against the clock, no help, no dictionary.

What the past papers show

  • The stories are easy to read: fables with a moral (a clever rabbit, a wise goat, an honest boy), funny stories with a twist (landing on the sun at night, "keep away from children"), and everyday stories (a haircut, a lost puppy, principal for a day).
  • About half the questions are find the fact questions. The rest ask why, how did they feel, what is the lesson, put in order, odd one out and word meaning.
  • Every paper since 2022 has at least one odd one out and one put the events in order question.
  • In 2025 the third story was a fill-in-the-blanks grammar story (verb forms, could and would, was and were). Skills 9 and 11 cover it.
  • The past papers did not test the rest of the official outline, but the outline is the rule book: timetables, messages and invitations, everyday vocabulary, spelling and polite expressions. Skills 11 to 13 cover those, and Papers 4, 6, 7 and 9 practise them.
  • Trap answers look like the story but say something slightly different, or add a detail the story never gives. One option is sometimes "It doesn't say in the story", and sometimes that is the right answer.

Exam rules to make automatic

  1. Read the whole story once, slowly, before looking at any question.
  2. For every question, go back and put your finger on the line in the story that proves the answer.
  3. Cross out options that are not in the story. Then cross out the ones that are in the story but do not answer the question.
  4. Watch for words like first, second, last, best, opposite, odd one out, NOT.
  5. Fill the bubble for the letter you chose and check the question number matches.
1

Find the Fact

Who, what, where, when, how old, how many. About half of every paper. The answer is written in the story, word for word or nearly.

How the paper asks it

  • "Where did Sam find the puppy?" (2024) · "What was the first thing Lily painted?" (2024)
  • "Who is Justin?" · "How old is Justin?" · "How old is Jen?" (2022)
  • "What game did Olivia play in the gym?" · "What was Olivia's second announcement?" (2023)
  • "What kind of seeds did the emperor give to the children?" (2025)
Trap. In 2022, "How old is Jen?" had the answer "It doesn't say in the story." Jen is called "his older sister" but no age is given. If you cannot put your finger on the number, that option is the right one.

How to do it

  1. Underline the question word: who, what, where, when, how many, which, first, last.
  2. Find the key word of the question in the story (puppy, seeds, gym) and read that whole sentence.
  3. Match the story's words to an option. Beware of options that use a story word in the wrong place: "Max ran off to chase a ball" sounds right, but the story says squirrel.
  4. "First" and "last" need the order: the first thing Lily painted was the butterfly, not the tree.
Worked example (2024). "The puppy licked Sam's face and started wagging its tail, which shows…" Find the line: he picked it up and it licked his face, wagging its tail. A licking, wagging puppy is happy. Answer: The puppy was happy to be rescued by Sam.

Mini quiz

1"Mia's brother Tom is seven. Mia is two years older." How old is Mia? A) 5 B) 7 C) 9 D) 11 E) It doesn't say

C. Seven plus two is nine. The number is not written, but the story gives enough to work it out.

2"Mia's brother Tom is seven. Mia is older." How old is Mia? A) 8 B) 9 C) 10 D) 12 E) It doesn't say

E. Older than seven could be any age. No number, no sum: it doesn't say.

3"Ali packed a torch, a rope and a map. Then he added an apple." What did Ali pack LAST? A) torch B) rope C) map D) apple E) water

D. "Then he added" tells you the apple came last. Water is never mentioned.

2

Why Questions

Why did she do that? Why did it happen? What would have happened if…? The answer is a reason, and the reason is in the story even when it is not in the same sentence.

How the paper asks it

  • "Why did the rabbit lead the lion to a well?" (2022) · "Why did the girl cut her hair herself?" (2023)
  • "Why did the children call the old man?" (2023) · "Why did Ling's pot remain empty?" (2025)
  • "Why do you think Justin believes the sun isn't hot at night?" (2022) means: what wrong idea is in his head?
  • "What would most likely have happened if the leopard had taken a step forward?" (2025) means: use what the goat said.
Clue words. Reasons hide behind because, so, so that, to, since, that's why. "I'm going to land on it at night" comes right after "I know it's too hot": the "that's why" links them.

How to do it

  1. Find the action in the story (the rabbit led the lion to the well).
  2. Read the sentences before and after it. The reason is usually there: the rabbit had told the lion about "another lion".
  3. Ask: does this option explain the action, or just repeat another fact from the story? "So that the lion could drink water" is a fact about wells, not the story's reason.
  4. For "what if" questions, use the rule the story gives: the goat said attackers turn to stone, so if the leopard stepped forward, he would have turned into stone is the answer the story wants.
Worked example (2023). "Why did the children call the old man?" The story says: "Let's have some fun with him!" and they planned a trick. So the reason is they wanted to have some fun, not "they had a serious problem" (they only pretended).

Mini quiz

1"Nadia put on her boots because the garden was muddy. Then she fed the hens." Why did Nadia put on boots? A) to feed the hens B) the garden was muddy C) it was cold D) her mother said so E) she liked them

B. The word "because" gives the reason straight away. Feeding the hens is what she did next, not why she wore boots.

2"The baker put a sign on the door: Back in 10 minutes. He ran to the bank before it closed." Why did the baker leave his shop? A) he was tired B) to buy flour C) to go to the bank D) to eat lunch E) the shop was closing for the day

C. The next sentence explains the sign. "Before it closed" is about the bank, not the shop.

3"If you touch my web, said the spider, the whole forest will hear a bell." What would happen if the fly touched the web? A) the fly would fly away B) the spider would sleep C) a bell would be heard D) the web would break E) nothing

C. Use the rule the story gives, even if it sounds magical.

Feelings and Character

How did he feel? What kind of person is she? Which word describes him best? The story shows feelings through actions and words; you name them.

How the paper asks it

  • "How did Sam feel when he couldn't find Max?" → "He felt sad and worried" (2024)
  • "How did Leo, Maya and Ben feel about their treasure?" → "Happy and content" (2024)
  • "What quality of Ling impressed the emperor the most?" → "His honesty" (2025)
  • "Which best describes the leopard's weakness?" → "He was easily scared of confident words" (2025)
  • "The old man …" → "pretended that he believed the children" (2023): kind and wise
Show, don't tell. Stories rarely say "he was worried". They say "Feeling sad, he sat down on a bench, worried he had lost his best friend." Actions are feelings in disguise: crying = sad, cheering = happy, shaking head and silent = disappointed.

Character words

honest (tells the truth), wise (makes good decisions), clever (quick thinker), brave (faces fear), patient (can wait), greedy (wants more than needed), kind, lazy, polite, rude, generous (gives freely), curious (wants to know).

Mini quiz

1"Zara opened the box, then shook her head and walked away without a word." How did Zara feel? A) excited B) disappointed C) proud D) relieved E) scared

B. Shaking her head and saying nothing shows disappointment, like the mother in the haircut story.

2"Bilal gave half his lunch to the new boy who had none." Which word describes Bilal? A) greedy B) lazy C) generous D) rude E) nervous

C. Giving freely is generous.

3"When the lights came back on, Dad let out a long breath and smiled." Dad felt… A) furious B) bored C) relieved D) jealous E) sleepy

C. The long breath and smile after a worry means relief.

4

The Lesson of the Story

What lesson does this story teach? What did she learn? Fables and most story endings carry a big idea. Pick the lesson that fits the WHOLE story, not one small part.

How the paper asks it

  • "What lesson did the girl learn?" → "Be patient" (2023, the haircut). The mother even says it: "All good things come to those who wait."
  • "What lesson does this story teach?" → "Taking care of our pets is important" (2024)
  • "What lesson can we learn from Lily's story?" → "Using our talents to help others is important" (2024)
  • "What lesson does the story teach?" → "Honesty and integrity are more valuable than results" (2025)
  • "What saved the rabbit from being eaten?" → "The rabbit's smart thinking" (2022): the lesson in disguise

How to do it

  1. Look at the last lines. Many stories say the lesson out loud: "Wisdom is stronger than muscle strength." "The real treasure is the joy of adventure and friendship."
  2. Ask: what did the main character do right, or wrong, and what happened because of it?
  3. Cross out lessons that are too small ("Do not cut your own hair") or too extreme ("Never go to the park again", "Magic can solve all problems").
  4. Cross out lessons that are good advice but not in this story ("Always say the truth" for the haircut story).
Story patternUsual lesson
Small clever animal beats big bullyBrains beat strength
Child rushes, it goes wrong, adult fixes itBe patient, listen
Everyone cheats except oneHonesty wins
Someone shares a gift or talentHelp others with what you have
Friends search and find nothing valuableFriendship is the real treasure

Mini quiz

1A crow drops pebbles into a jug until the water rises and it can drink. Lesson? A) Crows are thirsty B) Think and keep trying C) Never drink water D) Pebbles are heavy E) Jugs are useful

B. The crow thinks of a plan and does it step by step. A and D are facts, not lessons.

2A boy cries "Wolf!" for fun twice; the third time a real wolf comes and nobody helps. Lesson? A) Wolves are dangerous B) Shepherds should sleep C) If you lie, people stop believing you D) Never shout E) Villagers are lazy

C. The whole story is about the lie and its result.

3Lina the kangaroo tries to fly, fails, then teaches a joey to hop. What did Lina learn? A) Kangaroos can fly B) Koalas are rude C) Be proud of what you can do D) Never climb hills E) Birds are better

C. "Kangaroos can do amazing things too, like hopping high and fast."

Put Events in Order

Three or four numbered sentences, and you choose the option with the correct order, like 3/1/2. One per paper in 2022, two in 2023.

How the paper asks it

  • 2022: 1. The rabbit told the lion about another lion. 2. The lion drowned. 3. Animals decided to send one animal a day. Answer: 3/1/2.
  • 2023: 1. The old man tickled the feet. 2. The children wanted to play a trick. 3. The children put their feet in the water. Answer: 3, 2, 1.
  • 2023: Olivia: fair (2) → chair (4) → kindergarten (1) → no homework (3). Answer: 2, 4, 1, 3.
  • 2023 also used it for steps: a good haircut is bath, brush, cut: 3, 2, 1.

How to do it

  1. Do not read the options first. Decide the order yourself.
  2. Find the event that happened first in the story and write its number. Then the last. The middle is what is left.
  3. Now find the option that matches. If two options look the same (2023 had "2, 1, 4, 3" twice), check each number again.
  4. Watch out: the numbering in the question is never the story order. 1 is almost never first.
Time words help: first, then, next, after a while, finally, at the end of the day, on Monday morning, from that day on.

Mini quiz

1Story: Omar planted a seed. Weeks later a flower opened. Every day he watered it. Order these: 1. flower opened 2. planted a seed 3. watered it. A) 1,2,3 B) 2,3,1 C) 3,2,1 D) 2,1,3 E) 3,1,2

B. Plant, water, flower. "Weeks later" tells you the flower came last even though it was written second.

2Steps to make tea: 1. pour into a cup 2. boil water 3. add the tea leaves. A) 1,2,3 B) 2,3,1 C) 3,1,2 D) 2,1,3 E) 1,3,2

B. Boil, add leaves, pour.

6

Odd One Out

Five words from the story. Four belong to one group, one does not. At least one in every paper since 2022, sometimes two.

How the paper asks it

  • doctor, grandson, son, brother, grandfather → doctor (the others are family words) (2022)
  • a child, a girl, a boy, a lake, an old man → a lake (the others are people) (2023)
  • scissors, best friend, brush, hair, mirror → best friend (the others are haircut things) (2023)
  • principal, photocopier, spinning chair, chewing gum, gym → principal (the only person) (2023)
  • Goat, Stone, Leopard, Cave, Wolf (2025): a messy one. Three animals, two non-living things, so no clean group of four. Notice that Wolf is the only word that never appears in the story. When meaning gives no group of four, ask which word does not belong to the story.

How to do it

  1. Say what each word is: person, animal, place, thing, feeling, action.
  2. Find the group of four. The one left over is the answer.
  3. If all five are the same kind (all people), look for a finer group: family words vs job words, living vs non-living, things you use vs things you do.
  4. Do not pick the word you like least or the longest word. Pick the one that breaks the group.
Common groups. family members · jobs · animals · parts of the body · school things · kitchen things · feelings · colours · weather words · things that are alive · places · verbs (doing words).

Mini quiz

1A) teacher B) nurse C) farmer D) kitchen E) pilot

D. Four jobs and one place.

2A) mother B) uncle C) neighbour D) aunt E) cousin

C. Four family words and one person who is not family.

3A) run B) jump C) swim D) climb E) tired

E. Four doing words (verbs) and one describing word.

4A) sparrow B) eagle C) bat D) parrot E) crow

C. Four birds; a bat flies but is a mammal with fur, not a bird.

Word Meanings

Synonyms (same meaning), opposites, and "what do you think a ... is?" The story around the word tells you what it means.

How the paper asks it

  • "Choose the correct synonym for wise" → clever (2025)
  • "What is the opposite of polite?" → rude (2023)
  • "What do you think a prescription is?" → directions on how to take pills: the story shows a doctor's paper saying "Take 2 pills a day" (2022)
  • "Looking at a reflection in water is like looking…" → in a mirror (2022)
  • "What does an astronaut do?" → travels in a spacecraft (2022)
Use the story. For a hard word, read the sentence it sits in and the next one. "He pulls out a small piece of paper… It's a prescription for some pills that his doctor gave him." The meaning is right there.

Mini quiz

1"The giant was enormous." A synonym for enormous: A) tiny B) huge C) angry D) slow E) green

B.

2The opposite of "ancient": A) old B) big C) modern D) broken E) golden

C. Ancient means very old, so the opposite is new or modern.

3"Grandma keeps her glasses in a spectacle case so they don't scratch." A spectacle case is… A) a box for glasses B) a kind of mirror C) a pair of shoes D) a bag for books E) a cloth

A. The sentence tells you: glasses go in it, so they don't scratch.

Family and People Words

Who is whose son, brother, grandfather? Stories love three generations in one house, and the questions turn the family tree round and round.

How the paper asks it

  • "What is the relationship of David and Leo?" → Leo is David's son (2022)
  • "Who is Leo's son?" → David's grandson (Archie, named in another way) (2022)
  • "Who is Justin?" → Jen's brother: the story says "his sister Jen" (2022)
  • "Who is Max in the story?" → the puppy (2024). People words include pets' names.
Draw it. When a story has a grandfather, a father and children, draw three rows in the margin and write the names. Every relationship question becomes easy.

Words to know

Other people words: neighbour (lives next door), stranger (you do not know them), principal (head of the school), emperor (ruler of an empire), successor (the next one in the job), villager, passenger, customer.

Mini quiz

1"Sara's mother has a brother called Imran. Imran has a daughter, Hina." Who is Hina to Sara? A) sister B) aunt C) cousin D) niece E) grandmother

C. Your uncle's daughter is your cousin.

2"Mr Khan's son Asad has a baby boy." Who is the baby to Mr Khan? A) son B) nephew C) brother D) grandson E) cousin

D. Your son's son is your grandson.

3"Ten-year-old Zain and his twin, Zara, walked to school with their neighbour Mrs Ali." How old is Zara? A) 8 B) 9 C) 10 D) 11 E) It doesn't say

C. Twins are the same age.

Grammar Fill-ins

A story with numbered gaps, and five words for each gap. New in 2025 for the whole 5-point section, so it can be worth 40 points. Verb forms, could and would, was and were, joining words.

How the paper asks it (2025)

  • "wishing she …(17) join them" → could (wish + could)
  • "One day, Lina …(18) an idea" → got (past tense, like the rest of the story)
  • "collected big leaves and …(19) them to her arms" → tied (collected and tied, both past)
  • "spread her wings and …(20) into the air" → jumped
  • "but then she …(21) into a soft bush" → fell (not "had fell", which is wrong English)
  • "You're …(22) for hopping" → made (you are made for)
  • "a little joey who …(23) scared" → was (one joey)
  • "That day, Lina …(24) something important" → learnt
  • Also 2025 Q15: "The emperor smiled … Ling came forward" → when

Rules that answer most gaps

  1. Match the time of the story. Stories are told in the past: walked, saw, got, tied, jumped, fell, learnt. If the words around the gap are past tense, choose the past tense.
  2. Irregular past tense: go → went, see → saw, fall → fell, fly → flew, get → got, bring → brought, learn → learnt/learned, teach → taught, run → ran, swim → swam, eat → ate, give → gave, take → took, think → thought, find → found, feel → felt, make → made, come → came, sit → sat, write → wrote, hide → hid.
  3. was / were: one person or thing → was; more than one, or "you" → were. "The joey was scared." "The koalas were laughing."
  4. could / would / can / will: in a past story, "wished she could", "said he would". Can and will belong to the present and future.
  5. has / have / had: he, she, it → has; I, you, we, they → have; past → had.
  6. Joining words: when (time), because (reason), although (surprise: "although it rained, we played"), if (condition), unless (if not), so that (purpose), but (contrast), until (up to that time).
  7. -ing after prepositions and after "keep, enjoy, stop": "made for hopping", "kept trying".

Mini quiz

1"Yesterday Hamza … to the park and … a kite." A) goes / flies B) went / flew C) go / fly D) gone / flown E) will go / will fly

B. "Yesterday" means past: went and flew.

2"The twins … late, so they … running." A) was / start B) were / started C) is / starts D) were / starting E) was / started

B. Twins are two people: were. Past story: started.

3"We stayed inside … it was raining." A) although B) because C) unless D) so that E) until

B. The rain is the reason for staying in.

4"… it was raining, the children played outside." A) Because B) If C) Although D) Unless E) So

C. A surprise: rain, yet they played. That is "although".

5"Nadia wished she … swim like a fish." A) can B) could C) will D) must E) is

B. Wished + could, exactly like the kangaroo story.

Tricky Options

"Choose the correct sentence." "What is a better way of wording…?" "What mistake did he make?" Five options, and three of them sound almost right. This skill is about crossing out.

How the paper asks it

  • "Choose the correct sentence" (2023): only "Mom was disappointed that her daughter cut her own hair" matched the story. The others changed one detail each (longer hair, friend's hair not nice, result beautiful).
  • "What mistake did Grandpa David make?" (2022): he thought he had to keep away from children, but the pills had to be kept away.
  • "What is a better way of wording the warning?" (2022): "Keep these pills away from children."
  • "What fact is Justin missing?" (2022): the Earth rotates, so at night the sun is still shining on the other side.

The five kinds of wrong option

Wrong option typeExample from the papersHow to catch it
Not in the story"Because that's what his teacher told him"Can't find it: cross it out
In the story, but answers a different question"The lion roared loudly" for "what was evil?"Re-read the question
One word changed"chase a ball" instead of "chase a squirrel"Check every noun
Too extreme"never", "always", "all problems", "for sure"Be suspicious of absolute words
Opposite of the story"The result was beautiful when the girl cut her own hair"Remember how it really ended
Two left? Read both again with the story line under your finger. The right one matches the story and answers the exact question.

Mini quiz

1Story: "Ahmed forgot his umbrella, so he ran home in the rain and arrived soaking wet but laughing." Choose the correct sentence. A) Ahmed had an umbrella. B) Ahmed walked slowly. C) Ahmed was dry when he got home. D) Ahmed was wet and happy. E) Ahmed was angry.

D. A, B and C flip a fact; E is not in the story.

2A sign says "Dogs must be carried on the escalator." Tariq has no dog and waits at the bottom. What mistake did Tariq make? A) He should buy a dog. B) He thought everyone needed a dog; the sign means if you have a dog, carry it. C) He read the sign too fast. D) He should take the stairs. E) He is scared of escalators.

B. Same joke as the prescription: the warning is about the dog, not about the person.

Grammar Toolkit: Pronouns, Prepositions, Adverbs, Modals

The official outline lists these under "Word and Structure Knowledge". They appear inside fill-in-the-blank stories and in one-line questions.

Pronouns

KindWordsExample
Possessive (before a noun)my, your, his, her, its, our, theirThis is her bag.
Possessive (stands alone)mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirsThe bag is hers. Those books are mine.
Demonstrative, nearthis (one), these (many)This pen, these pens.
Demonstrative, farthat (one), those (many)That hill, those hills.
Object pronounsme, you, him, her, it, us, themGive it to him.
Trap. its = belonging to it (the cat licked its paw). it's = it is. your = belonging to you; you're = you are. their = belonging to them; there = a place; they're = they are.

Prepositions

Place words: in, on, under, behind, in front of, next to, beside, between, above, below, near, at, into, out of, through, over.

Time words: at 3 o'clock, at night · on Monday, on 14 March · in July, in the morning, in 2025.

Other useful ones: go to school, come from Lahore, play with a friend, afraid of dogs, good at maths, listen to music, wait for the bus, look at the board, arrive at the park.

Adverbs (how, when, where)

How: quickly, slowly, carefully, loudly, quietly, gently, happily, badly, well (not "goodly"), fast (not "fastly"), hard. Most are adjective + ly: quick → quickly, happy → happily, gentle → gently.

When / how often: yesterday, today, tomorrow, now, soon, later, always, usually, often, sometimes, never, once a week.

Where: here, there, outside, inside, upstairs, everywhere.

Test. An adjective describes a thing (a slow tortoise). An adverb describes an action (the tortoise walked slowly). If the word sits next to a verb, it usually needs -ly.

Simple tenses in one line

Every day he plays (present: he/she/it takes -s). Yesterday he played (past: -ed, or an irregular form such as went, saw, ate). Tomorrow he will play (future). The irregular verb list is in Skill 9.

Invitations, Notes, Timetables and Everyday Talk

The outline says reading includes "visual formats such as pictures and timetables" and "short texts like messages and invitations", and that spoken and written expression means greetings, apologies, introductions and requests.

Reading a short text

Every invitation or note answers the same five questions. Find each one and underline it.

QuestionLook for
Who?the name at the top or the signature at the bottom (From, Love, Dear ...)
What?party, picnic, match, message to do something
When?day, date, start time and end time
Where?address, "the house with the blue gate", room number
What to do?bring, wear, reply by (RSVP), phone

Reading a timetable

  • Read the headings first: what are the rows and what are the columns?
  • Put a finger on the row and a finger on the column. Where they meet is the answer.
  • "How long" questions: count from the start time to the end time. 4:00 to 5:15 is one hour and fifteen minutes.
  • "Which comes next after 3 pm?" means the first time after 3 pm, not the one nearest to it.

Ten full-length sample papers in the IKLC Ecolier format: three stories, 24 questions, options A to E, 3 points each for questions 1 to 8, 4 points for 9 to 16 and 5 points for 17 to 24. Read the story, answer its eight questions, then press Check paper for the score and a short explanation of every answer.

How to use them

  • Do them after the thirteen skills. One paper every day or two is a good pace; the last two against the clock.
  • Press Start timer for the 120-minute contest time. Most children finish in 40 to 60 minutes; use the rest to check each answer against the story.
  • Press Check paper. Green is right, red is wrong, and the line under each question explains why. Read every explanation.
  • A good score to aim for is 95 or more out of 120 (the official marking: 24 starting points, plus 3, 4 or 5 for each right answer, minus 1 for each wrong one). Write each score in the checklist above.
  • Papers 2, 5, 8 and 10 end with a fill-in-the-blanks grammar story, like the 2025 paper.
